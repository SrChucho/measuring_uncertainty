
Motivation: 
-------------
Alan Greenspan observed, "Uncertainty is not just an important feature of the monetary policy landscape; it is the defining characteristic of that landscape" (Greenspan, 2003).

Effects of heightened uncertainty: more dispersion and negative skewed future growth distribution; non-linear and asymmetric responses to uncertainty; higher asset volatility. 

Monetary policy conducts balance of risks weighing downside risks ---production, unemployment--- and upside risks--- inflation, to determine the entire probability distribution. 

Measuring Uncertainty: 
--------------------
Some concepts of uncertainty in the literature: 
EPU - (Baker, Bloom and Davis) How uncertainty is the Policy Environment that HH and firms face.
VIX - How much financial markets expect asset prices to fluctuate
JLN - (Jurado, Ludvigson and Ng) How unpredictable in macroeconomy
LMN - (Ludvigson, Ma and Ng) What are the roles of \textbf{macroeconomic} and \textbf{financial} uncertainty


Literature: 
-----------------------

Cebreros (2000) How trade policy uncertainty affects FDI.


Research question: what role does macroeconomic and financial uncertainty play in the determination of growth expectation of the Mexican economy?

contribution: combine methodologies for the Mexican economy 


Methodology:
------------
1. Estimate common trends to the Mexican economy, following Coronal et al (2020)\footnote{available at https://github.com/FranciscoL-B/ioae_assesment/}. They develop a panel of 26 variables that are used to nowcast Mexican monthly economic activity indicator (IGAE).

2. Compute \textit{macro} uncertainty, MU, and \textit{financial} uncertainty, FU, indicators as the unforseable component of economic variables after extracting all common predictable variation in the DFM.

3. Estimate a SVAR to identify causal direction between MU, FU and economic activity. 

4. Estimating the entire conditional distribution of growth for evaluating uncertainty’s impact in the Mexican economy.

Model
----------------------
Formally, let \mathbf{X}_t be the set of data, we estimate the DFM as
\begin{equation}\label{eqDFM}
\mathbf{X}_t=\mathbf{P} \mathbf{F}_t + \boldsymbol{\varepsilon}_t,
\end{equation}
We then extract the idiosyncratic component as
e_it = x_it − F_t′λ_i  
Using JLN estimate GARCH(1,1) per variable \rightarrow \sigma_it, and build 
MU_t = mean{ σ_it : i ∈ Macro }      (standardised)
FU_t = mean{ σ_it : i ∈ Financial }  (standardised)

Then, estimate SVAR: VAR([MU, FU, IGAE_growth])
       Ordering A: MU → FU → IGAE  (LMN baseline)
       Ordering B: FU → MU → IGAE  (robustness)



Data
----------------
The time series are of monthly frequency, starting from January 2004, with some available through January 2025. All variables have been obtained in seasonally adjusted form; if not, they have been adjusted using the X-13ARIMA-SEATS method. This seasonal adjustment relies on the automatic modeling procedure implemented in the \verb|seas| function from R’s \verb|seasonal| package. Table~\ref{TabVar} provides a summary of the dataset, including information on each variable’s availability up to $T+1$ and/or $T+2$, as well as the optimal transformation applied.
