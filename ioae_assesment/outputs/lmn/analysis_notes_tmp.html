1. Methodology in brief

The pipeline follows Jurado, Ludvigson & Ng (2015) — JLN — and Ludvigson, Ma & Ng (2021) — LMN — applied to the Mexican monthly macro-financial panel from Corona et al.

Raw panel (35 variables, 2004/01–present)
  │
  ├─ Seasonal adjustment: X-13 via seas()
  ├─ Stationarity: best_trans() — linear / monthly / annual growth
  │
  ▼
Scaled panel X  [T × N]
  │
  ├─ Bai-Ng IC → r = 7 factors
  ├─ DFM via nowcast() method="2s"
  │     fhat  [T × r]  common factors
  │     Phat  [N × r]  loadings
  │
  ▼
Idiosyncratic residual   e_it = x_it − F_t′λ_i   [T × N]
  │
  ├─ GARCH(1,1) per variable → σ_it  [T × N]
  │
  ▼
MU_t = mean{ σ_it : i ∈ Macro }      (standardised)
FU_t = mean{ σ_it : i ∈ Financial }  (standardised)
  │
  └─ Proxy SVAR: VAR([MU, FU, IGAE_growth])
       Ordering A: MU → FU → IGAE  (LMN baseline)
       Ordering B: FU → MU → IGAE  (robustness)

Key conceptual distinction: uncertainty here is the conditional volatility of the unforecastable component. The DFM strips out all predictable common variation first; GARCH then captures time-varying unpredictability in what remains. This differs from VIX (options-implied vol) and EPU (text-based), both of which conflate uncertainty with risk aversion or media salience.

Impulse response functions

90% bootstrap confidence bands, 500 replications. Two panels show how the IGAE response to MU and FU shocks changes under each Cholesky ordering. Ordering sensitivity is the key diagnostic — if the IGAE response to MU is very different across orderings, the identification is fragile and the full LMN rotation approach would be needed.

Forecast error variance decomposition — IGAE growth at 12-month horizon

Ordering MU share FU share Own (IGAE) share
A: MU first (LMN baseline) 0.008 0.020 0.972
B: FU first (alternative) 0.005 0.023 0.972

Interpretation: IGAE's own dynamics explain ~97% of its forecast error variance at 12 months. MU and FU together account for ~2.7%, which is small but consistent with several findings in the literature for small open economies. For Mexico, the dominant sources of output fluctuations are external demand shocks (US economy, oil prices) and domestic demand persistence — both absorbed into IGAE's autoregressive structure rather than into the uncertainty channel.

MU–FU correlation: 0.145 — the two indices are nearly orthogonal, which is desirable: they are capturing genuinely distinct dimensions of uncertainty rather than the same underlying phenomenon.

VAR lag order: SC selected p = 1 (AIC selected 4); the script uses SC capped at 4, giving p = 1.

Ordering sensitivity is mild: FU's share rises from 2.0% to 2.3% when it goes first, and MU falls from 0.8% to 0.5%. This suggests the two uncertainty channels are not cleanly causally ordered — motivating the full LMN rotation approach if causal identification is the primary goal.

6. DFM Factor Analysis

The DFM extracts r = 7 common factors from the 27-variable panel. These factors capture shared macroeconomic dynamics that are removed from each series before the GARCH step — they are the predictable common component. Understanding what each factor represents helps verify that the idiosyncratic residuals (which feed MU and FU) contain genuine uncertainty rather than systematic signal.

Factor time series

Seven panels show the time path of each factor over the 2005–2025 sample. All factors are sign-normalised so that F1 has positive correlation with IGAE growth. Peaks and troughs in the factor paths can be mapped to known economic events (2008–09 crisis, 2015–16 oil crash, 2020 COVID shock, 2022 inflation surge).

Factor loadings

The heatmap shows how each variable loads onto each factor. Blue = positive loading, orange = negative. Variables are ordered from most idiosyncratic (top) to least (bottom), matching the variance decomposition table in Section 3.

Reading guide:

  • Variables with large absolute loadings on a factor drive that factor's movements.
  • Variables near the top (high idiosyncratic share — INPC, GASOLINAS, confidence surveys) load weakly on all factors, which is why their uncertainty contribution is large: the factors don't absorb much of their variation.
  • Variables near the bottom (U_US, MANUF_USA, IPI_EUA) load heavily on one or two factors, reflecting their tight coupling with the US business cycle.

Important caveat: Factor labels are inferred from loadings and should be confirmed against the top-5 loadings table printed in the run log. Sign normalisation ensures F1 is cycle-aligned, but the other factors' signs are arbitrary.

Variance contribution per factor

Bars show each factor's individual contribution to total panel variance; the orange line is the cumulative total. Because the two-step principal-components estimator extracts factors in descending eigenvalue order, F1 always has the largest share.

Key insight: The cumulative line reaching ~67% at F7 is the 66.9% panel variance explained figure reported in Section 3. The remaining ~33% is in the idiosyncratic residuals — this is the raw material for MU and FU. A DFM that explained 90% or more would leave too little idiosyncratic variation for GARCH to estimate meaningful conditional volatility dynamics.
